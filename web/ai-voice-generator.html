What “AI voice” means here

The voices are neural text-to-speech voices from Microsoft's online speech service. A neural voice comes from a model trained on recorded human speech: it predicts how a text should sound, including stress and intonation, and synthesizes the audio, rather than stitching together recorded snippets. That is why it sounds much closer to a person reading than older computer voices.

Text 2 Speech is the free web page around that service. It splits your text into pieces, sends them with the voice, speed and pitch you chose, joins the returned audio and your pauses into one file, and builds subtitles from the timing of each spoken word. Each step is described on the how it works page.

Generate an AI voice in four steps

  1. Choose the language of your text in Voice Language, then click a voice; ▶ plays a sample of it.
  2. Type, paste or import up to 5,000 characters.
  3. Pick MP3 or WAV and, if you like, set speed, pitch and [break:…] pauses.
  4. Press Generate Audio. A text of the full 5,000 characters was ready in about 12 seconds in a test. Download the audio and, for captions, the SRT or VTT file.

Where an AI voice fits

  • Video voiceovers. Narration for explainers, tutorials, product demos and social clips, with captions from the same generation. The AI voiceover guide walks through a measured three-scene example.
  • E-learning and training. Lesson narration that is easy to update when the material changes: edit the sentence and generate again instead of booking a new recording.
  • Accessibility. An audio version of an article, notice or handout for people who prefer listening, plus subtitle files for viewers who cannot hear the audio. It does not replace a screen reader, which reads whole interfaces as you move through them.
  • Language practice. Hearing a sentence in the language and accent you are learning, slowed down if needed.
  • Drafts and prototypes. Placeholder narration and phone-menu or announcement prompts, to test the wording before a final recording.

To shape how the voice sounds (accent, speed, pitch and pauses), see text to voice.

How much text for how many seconds

In English tests at normal speed, the voice Aria (US) spoke about 15 to 16 characters per second, counting spaces and punctuation. That is enough to size a script before you write it:

Target lengthCharacters of scriptRoughly
15 seconds225–240Two or three sentences
30 seconds450–480A short paragraph
1 minute900–960Two paragraphs
5 minutes4,500–4,800Almost a full generation

Treat the table as a first estimate. Voices differ in pace by up to about a fifth, slower speeds stretch the audio, and pause markers add their own time, so generate the first scene and check its length in the results list before writing the rest.

Your text, your responsibility

Everything you convert is sent to Microsoft's online speech service, and the generated files are deleted from the Text 2 Speech server about two hours after your last generation. You are responsible for having the right to use the text and for how you use the audio: the terms of service apply, and Text 2 Speech gives no guarantee that a particular commercial use is allowed. Some platforms also ask creators to label synthetic or AI-generated voices, so check the rules of the place where you publish.

Limitations

  • One generation is limited to 5,000 characters and 25 pause markers.
  • About 20 requests per minute are allowed from one IP address, and the ▶ voice samples count toward that.
  • Voices depend on the speech service: one that stops answering is hidden from the list until it works again.
  • Results belong to the browser tab and expire; there is no account, history or saved project.
  • An internet connection is required, because the audio is made by an online service.
