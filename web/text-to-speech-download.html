Text to speech download: save speech as MP3 or WAV

Short answer: paste your text into Text 2 Speech, pick a voice and an output format, press Generate Audio, then click ⬇ MP3 (or ⬇ WAV) in the result to save the speech as a file. ⬇ SRT and ⬇ VTT save subtitles that match it. Downloading is free with no sign-up; each generation takes up to 5,000 characters, and the files stay online for about two hours.

How to download speech as an audio file

  1. Open the converter, set Voice Language to the language of your text and click a voice.
  2. Under Output Format, choose MP3 (the default) or WAV. Do this before generating: a finished result keeps the format it was made in.
  3. Paste or type your text, up to 5,000 characters, or import a plain-text file.
  4. Press Generate Audio and wait for the result to appear under Results.
  5. Play it once in the page to check names, numbers and pauses.
  6. Click the audio button to save the speech, and ⬇ SRT or ⬇ VTT if you need captions.
  7. Rename the files straight away, so you know later which text each one belongs to.

MP3 or WAV: which download to choose

Pick MP3 unless a program you use insists on WAV. Both hold the same sound, because the WAV is decoded from the generated MP3; the WAV is simply about eight times larger. Measured on the same 56.6-second clip:

MP3WAV
Size of the 56.6 s clip≈ 332 KB≈ 2.6 MB
Encoding48 kbps, 24 kHz, mono16-bit PCM, 24 kHz, mono
Best forSharing, web pages, e-learning platforms, phonesAudio and video editors, workflows that require PCM

The reasoning behind that advice, with byte-exact measurements, is in MP3 vs WAV for text to speech.

Download the subtitles as well

Each generation also produces two subtitle files, built from the moment each word is spoken: SRT for video editors and media players, VTT for video on web pages. There is nothing extra to switch on. A subtitle file only fits the audio of its own result, since another generation of the same text can be timed slightly differently, so keep each audio file together with its subtitles. Real examples of both formats are in the subtitles guide.

File names and where the files go

Your browser saves the files wherever it saves other downloads, usually the Downloads folder, unless it is set to ask you each time. The names are not taken from your text:

FileName when savedSuggested rename
Audio12 random letters and digits, for example c5983b36f65c.wavlesson-01.wav
SRT subtitlessubtitles.srtlesson-01.srt
VTT subtitlessubtitles.vttlesson-01.vtt

Because every result's subtitles share the same two names, a second download into the same folder is usually renamed by the browser, for example to subtitles (1).srt. Renaming each set as soon as it lands keeps the audio and its captions paired.

Downloading long texts

There is a limit: one generation takes at most 5,000 characters, counting spaces and pause markers, and Generate Audio stays disabled above it. In English that is about five and a half minutes of speech (5,000 characters became 5 minutes 28 seconds in a test), so longer pieces are downloaded as several files:

Length of the textFiles to generateApproximate audio in English
Up to 5,000 characters1Up to about 5½ minutes
12,000 characters3About 13 minutes
30,000 characters6About 33 minutes
55,000 characters11About 1 hour

Split at paragraph or chapter breaks, keep the voice, speed, pitch and format identical for every part, and number the downloads in order. The step-by-step method for joining the parts is in how to convert text to MP3.

How long downloads stay available

The files only live on the Text 2 Speech server for a short time: they are deleted about two hours after the last generation in your browser session. The results list belongs to the tab it was made in, so a different tab or window starts empty, and Delete removes a result and its files at once. There is no account or download history, which makes the copy on your own device the one that lasts.

Download problems

ProblemCause and fix
The download buttons are goneThe files expired or the tab was closed. Generate the text again and download at once.
Generate Audio cannot be pressedThe text box is empty or over 5,000 characters. Add text, or shorten and split it.
The audio button says WAV, not MP3WAV was selected when that result was made. Choose MP3 and generate again.
The WAV file is very largeThat is expected: about 2.9 MB per minute. Download MP3 for sharing.
Subtitles drift away from the audioThey come from a different generation. Take both files from the same result.

Limitations

  • 5,000 characters per generation, so long documents are saved as several files.
  • Audio comes as MP3 or WAV only; for another format, convert the download with an audio tool.
  • Files are not kept long-term and there is no history of past downloads.
  • The tool cannot open PDFs, Word files or web pages; copy their text into the box instead.
  • Your text is sent to Microsoft's online speech service to create the audio.
