How Text 2 Speech works

Text 2 Speech (text-2-speech.com) is a free online text-to-speech tool: you paste or type text, pick one of 300+ neural voices in 75 languages, and download the speech as an MP3 or WAV file together with SRT and VTT subtitles. This page explains what happens between pressing Generate Audio and getting the files.

Where the voices come from

The voices are Microsoft's online neural text-to-speech voices. On 31 October 2026 the catalog held 322 voices, 163 female and 159 male, across 75 languages and 142 regional variants, including twelve multilingual voices meant for text in more than one language. The server reloads the catalog once a day and tests every voice every six hours; a voice that fails eight test requests in a row is hidden until it works again. The voices and languages page shows the current list.

What happens when you press Generate Audio

  1. Your text is split at any [break:…] pause markers, and long passages are split at sentence ends into pieces of at most 1,500 characters.
  2. Each piece is sent to the speech service with the voice, speed and pitch you chose. Up to four pieces are synthesized at the same time, each request has 45 seconds to finish, and a piece that fails is retried once.
  3. The audio pieces and the pauses are joined in order into one MP3 file (48 kbps, 24 kHz, mono). If you chose WAV, that file is converted to 16-bit PCM WAV (24 kHz, mono).
  4. The speech engine reports when each word starts and ends. Those timings become the SRT and VTT subtitle files, with your original punctuation kept; if the engine sends no timings, they are estimated from the length of each word and the real duration of the audio.
  5. The result appears in your results list with a player and download links.

Why each generation has a limit

The 5,000-character limit, the cap of 25 pause markers and the per-minute request limit protect a free, anonymous service from abuse so that it stays fast for everyone. Within those limits a generation is quick: 5,000 characters of English became 5 minutes 28 seconds of audio in about 12 seconds when tested on 31 October 2026. Every limit and its exact value is listed in the help centre.

Where your text goes

Your text travels from your browser to the Text 2 Speech server and from there to Microsoft's online speech service, which returns the audio and the word timings. The server writes the finished files into a folder named after a random session identifier and deletes that folder about two hours after the last generation in it. The site sets no cookies and loads no third-party analytics or advertising scripts; the server only counts page views by date and page address. What is kept where is listed under data retention, and the privacy policy has the legal wording.
